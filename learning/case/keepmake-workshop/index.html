THE CONTEXT

A blank screen was also a useful starting question.

The workshop’s raw notes return to a basic difficulty: where does someone start when keeping a memory? Rather than resolve that question through discussion alone, the session asked the team to imagine an end result worth working toward.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I authored and sent the in-person agenda. Broader preparation and workshop ideas came from the team; the final record is collaborative.

DECISION 01

Start with a shared question, then give people room to make.

The agenda moved from opportunity statements and How Might We questions into individual sketches. Participants could draw, write, use Figma, or storyboard their ideal Year in Review. Different ways to contribute served the same concrete task.

DECISION 02

Put the end state on the table.

An ideal Year in Review gave the team an artifact to explain and compare. The gallery walk called for timed presentations, then themes and a Now / Next / Later view. Those were the planned activities; the retained notes show the ideas the team continued working through.

The original prompt allowed a physical sketch, an outline, a Figma design, or a short storyboard. It asked people to consider their own end-of-year memory review and break it into elements and features.

DECISION 03

Keep the original ideas visible through synthesis.

The workshop record retained raw problems and How Might We variations before later synthesis and user-story revisions. That separation matters: a team-generated idea needs to remain distinguishable from a finding established through user research.

Alternatives and constraints
Constraints and tradeoffs
Synthesis makes a large set of ideas usable, but merging too early can erase differences. Keeping the originals gives the team a way to revisit a theme.

Evidence behind this account

The source explicitly preserves repeats and near-repeats and distinguishes the workshop iteration from past research.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A workshop record the team could continue working with.

The retained agenda, workshop notes, and June follow-on comments show a concrete body of ideas and continuing collaboration. The output included problem statements, How Might We questions, sketches, and directions for further product work.

Leave room for people as well as activities.

A colleague’s request for a reset break reminded me to plan for people as carefully as activities. In future sessions, I would make breaks, transitions, and different ways to contribute explicit in the run of show.
