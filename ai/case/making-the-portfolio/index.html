THE CHALLENGE

AI can produce a polished-looking page that still tells the wrong story. Early versions relied too much on text and decorative imagery, while the real design journey was hard to see.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I set the visual and editorial direction, supplied missing originals, and turned feedback into concrete changes that AI-assisted implementation could act on.

01 / Observe

Start with what the page is failing to show.

The earlier AI page led with a small, text-heavy diagram. My feedback on the wider portfolio was specific: show the original problem, show the flow, and make the contribution easier to scan.

02 / Reusable practice

Give every asset a job.

My visual asset integration workflow separates documentary evidence from illustration. Original screenshots prove the work. Generated artwork can add identity or atmosphere, but it cannot stand in for a design I actually made.

Workflow, explained

  1. 01

    Anchor

    Name the exact screen region and the change it needs.

  2. 02

    Source

    Choose an original artifact or a clearly labeled illustration.

  3. 03

    Integrate

    Set the crop, hierarchy, caption, and responsive behavior.

  4. 04

    Inspect

    Review the rendered page and test the interaction.

The decision

An anchor is a precise visual reference, such as the recording control or a case-study cover. It turns “this feels off” into a change we can inspect.

03 / Refine

Make the change visible.

In the Design edition, the review loop separates the original interface, the redesign within the agreed scope, and the picture-in-picture proposal for future work.

WHERE IT LANDED

Something concrete to inspect.

The published portfolio now uses original screenshot sequences, short decision chapters, optional deeper notes, and source-aware image previews. This Applied AI edition extends that approach to the workflow behind the work.

What I learned and would test next

The most useful correction often removes something: an unsupported claim, a decorative asset without a purpose, or text that repeats what the screenshot already shows.

Next, test whether a recruiter can identify the problem, my contribution, and the result from a short scan of each case.

Original project screenshots and source documentation. Process diagrams are retrospective explanations.
