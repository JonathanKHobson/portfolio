Lumina3D / CREATIVE DEVELOPMENT · SPATIAL WORKFLOWS

Give an AI partner the world around the bug.

A cozy puzzle adventure became a practical lesson in creative direction, spatial context, and testing what an AI-assisted change actually does.

2 min read

Lumina3D
Original gameplay · local campaign build
My role
Creator · creative direction and AI workflow design
Timeframe
2026
Focus
Game experience, structured context, visual and interaction QA

Work shownLocal playable candidate · public source is an earlier version

View the public prototype source

THE CHALLENGE

“The bridge feels wrong” leaves too much to guess. In a 3D scene, a small error can depend on the actor, object, camera, collision, or current game state.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I shaped the game and a repeatable way to investigate it: capture the scene, name the objects, explain expected behavior, and verify a narrow change.

01 / Creative direction

Make a world worth exploring.

Lumina3D is a small puzzle adventure built around animal abilities and a trail of letters. The menu connects six chapters into one journey; the world gives each mechanic somewhere to belong.

02 / Reusable skill

Replace a vague request with usable context.

I documented a spatial debugging workflow that combines a screenshot with object IDs, runtime state, and expected behavior. That gives the AI partner enough context to propose a smaller, more reviewable fix.

Workflow, explained

  1. 01

    Observe

    Capture the scene and the unexpected behavior.

  2. 02

    Locate

    Attach object IDs, transforms, and runtime state.

  3. 03

    Constrain

    Ask for a narrow patch and a verification plan.

  4. 04

    Verify

    Revisit the scene and run the relevant checks.

The decision

A context packet is a structured bug brief. It makes the location and the expectation explicit instead of asking the model to infer them from a picture.

03 / Verification

Check the ending as carefully as the beginning.

The later local candidate connects the chapters through to the final letter. Build, route, and scene checks help catch broken transitions; a first-time human playtest is still a different test.

WHERE IT LANDED

Something concrete to inspect.

The local candidate has a six-chapter journey and a documented spatial debugging practice. The public repository preserves an earlier prototype; there is no current public playable release.

What I learned and would test next

AI-assisted implementation benefits from the same discipline as design: clear intent, stable references, small changes, and a check in context.

The next useful step is a first-time player test focused on wayfinding, puzzle clarity, and the complete journey.
