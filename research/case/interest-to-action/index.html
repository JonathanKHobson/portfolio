Find what stands between interest and action.

An audience survey showed why caring about an issue did not always lead to taking the next step. The findings helped shape a proposed Action Hub.

The question
What keeps an engaged audience from taking action, and what kind of support would help?
The evidence
1,459 completed submissions in the March 14, 2025 report snapshot; a voluntary audience sample.
The decision
Recommendations for a proposed Action Hub: clearer steps, reusable templates, reminders, and feedback about impact.

THE CONTEXT

Enthusiasm did not explain the effort of following through.

Oak and creator Qasim Rashid were exploring how to help an audience act on issues they cared about. The survey needed to distinguish several things that could look similar from a distance: paying attention, intending to act, finding a useful action, and following through.

The product context and constraints

I led survey design, launch, and analysis, bringing quantitative questions together with open responses. The report looked at audience context and platform behavior, reported obstacles, and interest in possible support. That gave the product discussion more to work with than a general expression of enthusiasm for a new tool.

The evidence supported an audience-specific direction. It did not tell us that the same people would use a new product repeatedly or pay for it. The useful output was a clearer set of problems to address and a narrower next experience to test.

Constraints that shaped the work

  • Recruitment reached a voluntary creator and advocacy audience, including people who did not directly follow Qasim.
  • The March 14 count was recorded while the survey was still live; it is a submission snapshot, not a final count of unique, quality-checked participants.
  • The retained report does not preserve each question's response base, complete wording, skip logic, or cleaning decisions.
  • Reported barriers and feature requests describe perceptions and preferences. They do not establish a causal effect on real-world action.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I led the survey work and synthesized the findings into recommendations for the Oak and creator collaboration. The findings report is a team artifact; the Action Hub was a proposed product direction, not a product outcome measured by this study.

DECISION 01

Ask about the work between attention and action.

The original objectives covered audience behavior, barriers, calls to action, and follow-through. Asking about those areas together made it possible to separate interest in a cause from the difficulty of deciding what to do next.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
A question about interest in an action-support tool was part of the report, but it could not stand in for the behavior and barrier questions. The research frame kept both the appeal of a possible tool and the circumstances of action in view.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The survey gave us a broad view of this audience's self-reports. It provided less detail about how a specific person moved through a real action from start to finish. That remained a question for follow-up research.

Evidence behind this account

DECISION 02

Distinguish a hard choice from a shortage of resources.

The report's largest listed barrier was decision fatigue: too many issues, calls to action, or steps to work through. Money, time, unclear instructions, and doubts about effectiveness appeared alongside it. Those obstacles suggested different kinds of support.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Treating every obstacle as a need for more information would miss the distinction. A clear instruction could help someone understand a step, while limited time or money could still prevent them from taking it.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The percentages made the reported pattern easy to see, but the retained report does not include a response base for each item. I would use the pattern to frame candidate improvements, not to estimate how many people in a wider population had each need or to calculate how much a feature would help.

DECISION 03

Follow one finding into a testable next step.

The report connects reported obstacles with requests for practical support. Here is one path through that evidence: unclear action steps, requests for templates and simpler instructions, and a focused first-action flow I would test next.

  1. 01 Reported barrier

    35%: unclear action steps

    The report describes a need for specific, easy-to-follow instructions. This is one of five reported barriers; its per-item response base is unavailable.

  2. 02 Requested support

    Templates and simpler steps

    The report also summarizes requests for pre-filled outreach templates and simplified action steps. These requests suggest support worth exploring.

  3. 03 My proposed next test

    One action, with enough help to start.

    I would test a focused outreach task with an editable template. The point is to see whether someone can move from understanding the request to taking the first step.

    1. Choose a relevant action and explain what it involves.
    2. Adapt the template accurately to their situation.
    3. Complete the first step, then identify what confirmation or support they need.

Connecting these two report sections is my interpretation; it does not establish that the same respondents selected both. The first-action flow is a proposed next test, not a tested interface or a measured Action Hub result.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
The next product discussion could focus on a specific action a person could start and finish, with enough support around it. A larger catalogue of information would not, by itself, answer the reported difficulty of choosing and following through.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The requested support addressed some parts of the problem more directly than others. A template could reduce the effort of starting a message; it would not remove a financial constraint or prove that the message had an effect. Those limits mattered when defining what the proposal could reasonably promise.

Evidence behind this account

The original report contains the barriers and requested features. This trace is a portfolio synthesis linking them; it is not an original ranked requirements matrix, a committed roadmap, or evidence that these features changed behavior.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A product direction grounded in the effort of acting.

The study produced an audience findings report and recommendations for a proposed Action Hub. It connected the audience’s reported obstacles to specific forms of support the team could explore: a clear step, a useful template, a reminder, and visible impact.

A feature request is the start of a hypothesis.

The most useful next step would be to take one of the proposed supports into a real task. I would begin with choosing and starting an action, then observe where the person hesitates or needs a different kind of help. I would also keep the report's financial barrier in view, even though it does not lead as neatly to a template or reminder feature.

The next question I would test

Use a focused action flow with intended audience members. Check relevance, comprehension of the commitment, completion of the first step, and what evidence of impact they expect. Recover the instrument and item bases before making comparisons between audience segments.
