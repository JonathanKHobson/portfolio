Keep the recording moving.

A revised recording sequence, a practical split-screen fix, and a picture-in-picture direction I kept open for the future.

3 min read

FactorLab · SmartTagIt
My camera flow designs · future PIP proposal shown in the case
My role
UX Designer & Researcher (Contract)
Timeframe
April 2026
Focus
Capture flow, product negotiation, engineering handoff

Work shownOriginal design flow and April 2026 handoff. PIP was future scope; implementation was not verified.

THE CHALLENGE

People needed to record a video, review an observation, and choose what to do next. In a stakeholder demo, Submit and camera actions competed for attention.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I redesigned the recording states, advocated keeping the existing review actions, and negotiated a smaller immediate fix while preserving a more flexible PIP direction.

01Before and redesign02Capture flow03Future PIP+Where it landed

01 / Original / scoped design

Untangle the controls from the form.

The original recording view put camera controls across the middle, with Submit still available in two places. My scoped redesign moved the recording controls to the bottom and removed the competing footer Submit.

Consistency and standards

I used familiar red recording controls and grouped related actions, so people could bring their camera experience with them. With product, I kept the split-screen structure and narrowed the immediate change to the competing footer controls.

02 / Broader design exploration

Connect recording to review.

I redesigned the recording states as a connected sequence and retained the existing preview, Retake, and Use Video actions. The wider exploration went beyond the smaller change agreed for the immediate handoff.

Visibility and user control

A timer and a distinct Stop control make recording status visible. Retake and Use Video preserve a choice before committing. I advocated keeping that review step when its removal entered the scope discussion.

03 / Future proposal

Give the form more room.

I proposed a movable picture-in-picture camera view so someone could keep recording while reviewing the form. This is the actual future-scope design I shared.

A hypothesis worth keeping

A movable camera view could leave more room for the form. I explained that benefit, acknowledged the team’s delivery constraints, and separated the proposal from the immediate handoff.

Decision notes and evidence

WHERE IT LANDED

A usable handoff without losing the larger idea.

On April 14, I handed over the revised controls and a separate PIP proposal. The discussion preserved existing preview functionality and made the boundary between the immediate change and future work explicit.

What I learned and would test next

Approval to investigate a problem is different from approval to implement a particular design. I learned to make that boundary explicit in the handoff.

Test recording, form review, and attachment as one task. For PIP, check whether people can move the camera away from required fields and still recognize the recording state.

Original design exports and April product correspondence. The problem came from a stakeholder demo. Implementation and post-change user impact remain unverified.
