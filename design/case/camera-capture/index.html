A clearer recording flow within a fixed scope.

Diagnosing competing camera and submit actions, advocating for a future picture-in-picture view, and separating it from the immediate fix.

Record media while reviewing an observation, then return to the right next action.

Where it landed
An April 14 revised design, with picture-in-picture documented separately for future consideration.

THE CONTEXT

A demo exposed a specific interaction problem.

The stakeholder’s recording showed difficulty completing an observation. Before changing the screens, I wrote a diagnosis of how Submit, media entry, and camera controls were competing. That gave the conversation a concrete problem to solve.

The product context and constraints

I explored a larger camera revision, but the engagement required a smaller change within existing engineering scope. I had to keep advocating for clearer behavior while adjusting the amount of change the team would take on.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I analyzed the demo, explained the problems and alternatives, and revised the mockups through product feedback. Product leadership set the immediate scope. The final handoff separated that scope from the longer-term direction.

INSIDE THE WORK

01 Resolve the competing actions first.02 Give the immediate fix and future idea separate handoffs.Outcome & reflection

DECISION 01

Resolve the competing actions first.

The key View Details change was to remove the bottom Submit and use familiar camera controls there instead. I retained the split-screen structure the product lead requested, keeping the correction focused on the confusing state.

Alternatives and constraints
Constraints and tradeoffs
The narrower layout kept the form and video competing for space. I still preferred exploring picture-in-picture, but the immediate design could address the control conflict without requiring that larger change.

Preserve useful existing behavior

When preview removal entered the discussion, I pointed out that preview, Retake, and Use Video already existed. Product clarified that they should remain.

DECISION 02

Give the immediate fix and future idea separate handoffs.

I proposed a draggable picture-in-picture view to leave more room for the form. Product kept the split-screen approach in scope. I revised the design and delivered separate walkthroughs for the current change and the future concept.

Retrospective summary of the original design discussion. The future direction was a proposal, not a validated or released improvement.

Alternatives and constraints
Constraints and tradeoffs
An early email described the broader direction as approved before product had agreed to it. I corrected that message and separated the next delivery into two walkthroughs. That made the scope of the requested engineering change explicit.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A narrower design, a clearer handoff.

I delivered the revised control treatment on April 14, with the larger picture-in-picture idea separated as future scope. The product discussion also preserved existing preview functionality after I questioned whether it should be removed.

Advocacy includes knowing what to carry forward.

I could defend the reason for a larger change and still deliver a useful smaller one. The lesson was to separate agreement on the problem from agreement on the implementation, then make that distinction visible in the files and walkthroughs.

The next question I would test

Observe the complete recording task, especially the transition into View Details and back. Compare picture-in-picture only if the team chooses to pursue that direction.

Original April design and contemporaneous product discussion. This is a design-and-handoff case; implementation and post-change usability were not verified. The recorded problem came from a stakeholder demo, not a formal participant study.
