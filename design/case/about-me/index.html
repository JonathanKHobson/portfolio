FactorLab · SmartTagIt / INFORMATION DESIGN · AI FEEDBACK

Make feedback useful before making it a score.

Turning a broad personal-dashboard concept into a feasible first release, with clearer summaries, useful detail, and negotiated scope.

5 min read

FactorLab · SmartTagIt
Original mobile design exploration
My role
UX Designer & Researcher (Contract)
Timeframe
2026
Focus
Information design, interaction flows, research-informed iteration, product and engineering collaboration

Work shownDesign iterations · team reported web/server release September 2 and mobile release September 3, 2026

THE CHALLENGE

A personal dashboard had to turn dense safety-conversation information into useful next steps. Stakeholder ambition, engineering capacity, and understandable feedback did not always point to the same design.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I explored the broader direction, brought research and customer context into review, and worked with product and engineering to choose a practical first release.

01 / Earlier experience / broader concept

From repeated cards to a connected overview.

The earlier page repeated comparison cards. My broader concept connected a personal summary, recommendations, and questions so the page could suggest a useful next step.

Recognition, not guesswork

The direction puts useful next actions beside the summary. People should not have to translate a score into a task on their own.

02 / Scope and collaboration

Agree on first-release priorities.

With product, I prioritized summaries, recommendations, the existing scoring view, comparisons, and accomplishments. The exploration below helped us discuss that direction; customization remained a separate scope decision.

Progressive disclosure

Start with a useful summary, then let someone inspect the detail when they need it. I worked with the team to preserve that path within a feasible first release.

Summary of the design and scope decisions. Personal widget customization remained an exploration.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored dense, simple, and milestone-focused versions, plus adding, removing, and reordering widgets. Another specific choice was whether recommended questions should have equal emphasis or one primary suggestion with quieter alternatives below it.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The product discussion favored more reusable modules so different activity measures and facts would not need a separate card design each time. At the same time, a cleaner presentation could not discard the detail people already had: Simple 7 bars still needed to lead to an explanation and recommended video.

Evidence behind this account

The April–May walkthroughs show the explored directions. The later team discussion supports the modularity and transition requirements. These sources do not prove that personal reordering, all density variants, or all proposed card types were included in the first release.

A concrete constraint

The video card could not rely on a duration the system did not have. The proposed revision removed that value and used a short description to help explain the recommendation.

03 / Implementation review

Keep the information engineering already had.

My accomplishments grid omitted details present in engineering’s table. I recommended keeping the table and refined the labels to distinguish activity given from activity received.

Adapt the design to better evidence

Engineering’s table retained information my grid missed. I recommended the stronger structure and clarified its labels instead of defending the first mockup.

Decision notes and evidence
Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Keep the smaller grid and add its missing measures, or adopt the table already developed by engineering. I recommended the table.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The revised rows distinguish likes and comments given from those received. I also proposed a link into activity from people the user followed. Product deferred that addition, keeping the immediate change focused on the existing information.

Evidence behind this account

The August review and product replies document the table decision, label work, and deferred link. The displayed design retains that historical link, so it is not presented as an exact release screenshot.

My contribution

Recognize a gap in my own design, recommend the stronger structure, and make its labels clearer.

04 / Content decision

Let people interpret the comparison.

A lower mention count is not automatically better. I advocated neutral labels and asked what the comparison group and each number actually represented.

Clarity without judgment

A comparison should explain what differs without implying that a person has performed badly. I questioned the labels and comparison context so the feedback could be interpreted fairly.

Decision notes and evidence
Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored Simple 7 against a zone or peer baseline, topic frequency against peers, and broader percentile comparisons. I also asked whether comparisons should sit in one compact section or inside the relevant cards.
Constraints and tradeoffs
I was aiming for neutral feedback that left the final judgment with the person. Product feedback also emphasized recognizing positive performance and comparing people at similar milestones. Those aims raised different questions about which metrics to show, who the comparison group was, and how much explanation the interface needed.

Evidence behind this account

The original sources support the alternatives and the discussion. They do not establish one final comparison policy or show that users interpreted the displayed samples correctly.

The question behind the chart

Compared with whom, over what period, and what should this difference help me decide?

WHERE IT LANDED

A first release shaped through design and engineering.

I took the work from broad page concepts through detailed flows and implementation review. The team reported About Me live on web and server on September 2, followed by both mobile apps on September 3, 2026. My contribution was making the choices concrete: what to reuse, what to clarify, and what to leave for later.

What I learned and would test next

A polished chart can hide an unresolved product question. Defining the comparison, its source, and how people should interpret it belongs in the design work from the start.

I would ask people to explain a comparison in their own words, find what it is based on, and describe a useful next action.
