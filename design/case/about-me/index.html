FactorLab · SmartTagIt / INFORMATION DESIGN · AI FEEDBACK

Make feedback useful before making it a score.

Turning an ambitious personal-insights concept into a feasible first release, with useful feedback, familiar interactions, and room to grow.

9 min read

UX Designer & Researcher (Contract)

Focus

Work shownDesign iterations · team reported web/server release September 2 and mobile release September 3, 2026

The task
Understand my recent safety conversations, inspect a comparison, and follow a useful recommendation.
My contribution
Page hierarchy, widget and interaction alternatives, research-informed decisions, and design-to-engineering refinement.
Where it landed
A team-reported web and mobile release in September 2026. The images show design iterations rather than a production walkthrough.

THE CONTEXT

A larger vision, a first release the team could build.

The page brought together personal summaries, recommendations, comparisons, and accomplishments. The challenge was to make that information useful while fitting the data and engineering capacity already available. I explored a richer experience, then worked with product and engineering to shape a practical first release.

The product context and constraints

Direct participant studies were outside the agreed scope of my work on this release. I drew on heuristic evaluation, established interaction patterns, and customer context relayed through product leadership. Short walkthroughs and asynchronous reviews helped us turn that context into specific design decisions. Those inputs informed the work without replacing direct usability validation.

Early explorations included denser and simpler pages, milestones, and personal widget controls. As the team clarified scope, I concentrated on stable content groups and familiar paths into existing summaries, video playback, and answers. The work included advocating for useful behaviors and changing my own designs when the implementation exposed a better option.

Constraints that shaped the work

  • The design needed to preserve useful existing capabilities, including historical summaries, the video player, and Simple 7 detail with recommended videos.
  • Content varied by metric and configured zone; the team wanted reusable card structures that could hold different facts.
  • Some metadata was unavailable: engineering asked to remove video duration and make room for a short description instead.
  • Comparison baselines, the role of positive feedback, and how much customization belonged in the first release were active product questions.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I designed the page directions and interactions, explained alternatives through annotated mockups and walkthroughs, and reviewed the build. Product leadership brought customer context and set priorities. Engineering clarified available data and implemented the experience. Our reviews shaped both the interface and the scope of the first release.

DECISION 01

Let a short summary open a deeper path.

The team wanted the most recent summary condensed into two or three useful sentences, with access to the full summary and earlier summaries. That meant the top of the page needed to offer both a quick reading and a way to inspect more.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Early exploration included an overall score or simpler statistics. Later review focused the summary on a short account of what mattered, while keeping more detailed measures in their own sections.
Constraints and tradeoffs
Reusing horizontal summary history preserved an existing capability, but its discoverability needed attention. In review, I pointed out that I kept rediscovering and forgetting the swipe interaction. That was my own observation during the work, not a usability-study result.

Evidence behind this account

The original discussion and engineering walkthrough identify summary history, the full-summary destination, and the proposed loading state for condensation. They establish the intended flow, not that people could discover it unaided.

The route after a recommendation mattered just as much.

  3. 03

    Ask a follow-up

    Continue the conversation when more detail is useful.

  4. 04

    Return to About Me

    Go back to the personal insights page.

For a suggested AI question, the team wanted the tap to carry the question into the existing answer experience instead of making someone type it again. The person could read the answer, ask a follow-up, then return to About Me. A video recommendation should use the familiar player and return path.

I also raised a limit to moving everything into chat: some trends might be more useful on the insights page first, with a follow-up question available afterward. A conversation was one route to detail; the team still needed to decide when the graphic itself belonged on the page.

DECISION 02

Make the categories stable and the content flexible.

I organized the page around five questions: what happened, what could help next, what the existing measures mean, how to interpret a comparison, and what progress to recognize. Keeping those groups stable made it easier to negotiate the content and behavior inside each one.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored dense, simple, and milestone-focused versions, plus adding, removing, and reordering widgets. Another specific choice was whether recommended questions should have equal emphasis or one primary suggestion with quieter alternatives below it.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The product discussion favored more reusable modules so different activity measures and facts would not need a separate card design each time. At the same time, a cleaner presentation could not discard the detail people already had: Simple 7 bars still needed to lead to an explanation and recommended video.

Evidence behind this account

The April–May walkthroughs show the explored directions. The later team discussion supports the modularity and transition requirements. These sources do not prove that personal reordering, all density variants, or all proposed card types were included in the first release.

A concrete constraint

The video card could not rely on a duration the system did not have. The proposed revision removed that value and used a short description to help explain the recommendation.

DECISION 03

Engineering’s table changed my design.

My accomplishments grid looked cleaner, but it left out information that engineering’s table already included. I recommended their more complete structure, then revised the row labels and Figma design. Preserving useful information mattered more than preserving my layout.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Keep the smaller grid and add its missing measures, or adopt the table already developed by engineering. I recommended the table.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The revised rows distinguish likes and comments given from those received. I also proposed a link into activity from people the user followed. Product deferred that addition, keeping the immediate change focused on the existing information.

Evidence behind this account

The August review and product replies document the table decision, label work, and deferred link. The displayed design retains that historical link, so it is not presented as an exact release screenshot.

My contribution

Recognize a gap in my own design, recommend the stronger structure, and make its labels clearer.

DECISION 04

A ranking needs a reference point and a reason to matter.

In the May 15 walkthrough, I questioned whether a top-percentile label supplied enough context and whether being near the top should automatically read as good. The topic-comparison exploration made the problem tangible: mentioning fatigue less often than peers does not explain why that difference exists.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored Simple 7 against a zone or peer baseline, topic frequency against peers, and broader percentile comparisons. I also asked whether comparisons should sit in one compact section or inside the relevant cards.
Constraints and tradeoffs
I was aiming for neutral feedback that left the final judgment with the person. Product feedback also emphasized recognizing positive performance and comparing people at similar milestones. Those aims raised different questions about which metrics to show, who the comparison group was, and how much explanation the interface needed.

Evidence behind this account

The original sources support the alternatives and the discussion. They do not establish one final comparison policy or show that users interpreted the displayed samples correctly.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

A first release shaped through design and engineering.

I took the work from broad page concepts through detailed flows and implementation review. The team reported About Me live on web and server on September 2, followed by both mobile apps on September 3, 2026. My contribution was making the choices concrete: what to reuse, what to clarify, and what to leave for later.

A feasible release still needs a clear experience.

The most useful design work often happened between versions. I had to distinguish a meaningful user need from a preferred layout, explain the cost of an alternative, and preserve a longer-term direction without making it a requirement for the first release. The grid-to-table decision is one example: collaboration improved the design when I was willing to change it.

The next question I would test

Ask people to interpret a comparison, find its reference group, open a recommendation, and return to the page. I would also check whether the accomplishments labels and summary-history route make sense without an explanation.
