THE CONTEXT

The page needed to connect the information to something a person could do.

About Me brought together several different kinds of information: a summary of recent activity, recommendations, Simple 7 dimensions, comparisons, and accomplishments. Some explained what had happened. Others suggested a question, a video, or a closer look. My job was to give those different purposes a clear structure.

The product context and constraints

The first explorations were deliberately broad. In April, I sketched a personal snapshot, stats, motivational elements, and ideas for the user, while asking what information engineering could support. By May, I was comparing a denser page, a simpler page, and a version with more emphasis on milestones and streaks. Customization was a direction to explore, with its place in the first release still open.

Review with the product and engineering team made the next problem more specific: what happens after a tap? A recommendation could open a video, trigger a suggested AI question, or reveal the meaning of a Simple 7 dimension. Each needed a destination and a clear route back.

Constraints that shaped the work

  • The design needed to preserve useful existing capabilities, including historical summaries, the video player, and Simple 7 detail with recommended videos.
  • Content varied by metric and configured zone; the team wanted reusable card structures that could hold different facts.
  • Some metadata was unavailable: engineering asked to remove video duration and make room for a short description instead.
  • Comparison baselines, the role of positive feedback, and how much customization belonged in the first release were active product questions.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I developed the page directions, widget structures, visual alternatives, and comparison questions. The product lead and engineers clarified the existing capabilities, data availability, configurable content, and required transitions. The release was a team outcome; these sources do not establish that every explored widget or customization feature shipped.

DECISION 01

Let a short summary open a deeper path.

The team wanted the most recent summary condensed into two or three useful sentences, with access to the full summary and earlier summaries. That meant the top of the page needed to offer both a quick reading and a way to inspect more.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Early exploration included an overall score or simpler statistics. Later review focused the summary on a short account of what mattered, while keeping more detailed measures in their own sections.
Constraints and tradeoffs
Reusing horizontal summary history preserved an existing capability, but its discoverability needed attention. In review, I pointed out that I kept rediscovering and forgetting the swipe interaction. That was my own observation during the work, not a usability-study result.

Evidence behind this account

The original discussion and engineering walkthrough identify summary history, the full-summary destination, and the proposed loading state for condensation. They establish the intended flow, not that people could discover it unaided.

The route after a recommendation mattered just as much.

  1. 01

    Choose a recommendation

    Tap a suggested AI question on About Me.

  2. 02

    Read the answer

    Carry the question into the existing answer experience.

  3. 03

    Ask a follow-up

    Continue the conversation when more detail is useful.

  4. 04

    Return to About Me

    Go back to the personal insights page.

For a suggested AI question, the team wanted the tap to carry the question into the existing answer experience instead of making someone type it again. The person could read the answer, ask a follow-up, then return to About Me. A video recommendation should use the familiar player and return path.

I also raised a limit to moving everything into chat: some trends might be more useful on the insights page first, with a follow-up question available afterward. A conversation was one route to detail; the team still needed to decide when the graphic itself belonged on the page.

DECISION 02

Make the categories stable and the content flexible.

The five families gave the page a structure the team could review: summary, recommendations, Simple 7, comparisons, and accomplishments. Within those groups, the visual explorations made the amount and kind of information concrete.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored dense, simple, and milestone-focused versions, plus adding, removing, and reordering widgets. Another specific choice was whether recommended questions should have equal emphasis or one primary suggestion with quieter alternatives below it.
Constraints and tradeoffs
The product discussion favored more reusable modules so different activity measures and facts would not need a separate card design each time. At the same time, a cleaner presentation could not discard the detail people already had: Simple 7 bars still needed to lead to an explanation and recommended video.

Evidence behind this account

The April–May walkthroughs show the explored directions. The later team discussion supports the modularity and transition requirements. These sources do not prove that personal reordering, all density variants, or all proposed card types were included in the first release.

A concrete constraint

The video card could not rely on a duration the system did not have. The proposed revision removed that value and used a short description to help explain the recommendation.

DECISION 03

A ranking needs a reference point and a reason to matter.

In the May 15 walkthrough, I questioned whether a top-percentile label supplied enough context and whether being near the top should automatically read as good. The topic-comparison exploration made the problem tangible: mentioning fatigue less often than peers does not explain why that difference exists.

Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
I explored Simple 7 against a zone or peer baseline, topic frequency against peers, and broader percentile comparisons. I also asked whether comparisons should sit in one compact section or inside the relevant cards.
Constraints and tradeoffs
I was aiming for neutral feedback that left the final judgment with the person. Product feedback also emphasized recognizing positive performance and comparing people at similar milestones. Those aims raised different questions about which metrics to show, who the comparison group was, and how much explanation the interface needed.

Evidence behind this account

The original sources support the alternatives and the discussion. They do not establish one final comparison policy or show that users interpreted the displayed samples correctly.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

Concrete layouts for a personal insights experience.

The design work gave the team a family of insight layouts to review alongside questions about the comparisons, their sources, and useful next actions. About Me’s web and server changes were released on September 2, 2026.

Design the click after the card.

The page became a more useful design problem when we worked through the transitions. A clean card still leaves work unfinished if the person cannot tell what opens, why an answer is relevant, or how to return. I would bring the comparison definitions and the routes into detail into the first review, alongside the page layout.

The next question I would test

Ask people to interpret a comparison, explain its reference group, open one recommendation, and return to the page. Check whether the short summary helps them choose a next action and whether they notice that earlier summaries are available.
