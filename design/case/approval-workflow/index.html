Give an observation a path to action.

Extending an existing observation flow so people can raise an issue, request approval, and understand what needs to happen next.

THE CONTEXT

The next step belonged inside the observation.

SmartTagIt already let people create observations with questions and media. The new need was to act on what an observation revealed: report an issue or request approval. I had to add that responsibility without treating it as a separate product or rebuilding the whole observation flow.

The product context and constraints

Product leadership supplied the use cases and priorities. I turned them into mobile flows and reviewed the developing implementation with engineering. We worked through creation, assignment, editing, follow-up, and the distinction between a question and its parent session.

Constraints that shaped the work

  • Extend familiar observation behavior within the first mobile phase.
  • Share form patterns while preserving task-specific requirements and configurable statuses.
  • Keep existing comments and media available, and distinguish workflow approval from an existing permit answer or signature.

MY CONTRIBUTION & COLLABORATION

I designed and refined the mobile concept, explained its behavior in Figma and Loom, and compared editing alternatives with the team. Engineering built the workflow. Product leadership set the scope, and our implementation reviews shaped the next corrections.

DECISION 02

Share the structure, distinguish the task.

An open issue and an approval request needed many of the same fields. I used a common structure while making their purpose, required inputs, status, and primary action explicit.

Original September 8 design captures with empty demonstration inputs.

View the full original recommendation
Alternatives and constraints
Alternatives
Design independent forms for each task, or share their field structure and vary the parts that carry meaning.
Constraints and tradeoffs
Reuse helped keep the workflow coherent, but it did not mean identical validation. In the shown approval design, Assigned to is required; the issue design does not mark it required.

The design choice

Reuse the structure. Make ownership, status, and the next action specific to the task.

DECISION 03

Work through the details after the first mockup.

Implementation review brought new questions about editing, existing comments and media, and what the workflow should communicate in a report. I translated those questions into specific alternatives and follow-up designs.

OUTCOME & REFLECTION

From a mobile concept to a released core workflow.

I delivered the mobile design and walkthroughs, then refined the handoff through implementation review. Engineering reported the core workflow live on September 23, 2026, with existing Open Issue data migrated. Email and report integrations were still being completed. My later report mockups reached engineering review the following week.

Design continues through implementation.

The first mockup made the idea discussable. Later reviews made the boundaries clearer: which behavior could be reused, which changes belonged in the first phase, and what needed to carry across desktop and reports. Those conversations were part of the design work.

The next question I would test

Observe whether people can recognize the scope of an issue, find their assignment, and change its status without confusing it with the parent observation or permit approval.
