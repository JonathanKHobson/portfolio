THE THREAD THROUGH MY WORK

I started by listening
to people’s stories.

A good question can change
the whole story.

I came to UX through documentary filmmaking. Listening closely, asking a better question, and deciding what belongs in the story were part of that work. I still use those habits when I look at a product: what is the person trying to understand, and what detail will help them move forward?

My background also includes technical communication, instructional design, and UX research. In the work shown here, those interests meet in small decisions: whether a saved question keeps its context, whether a comparison explains its reference point, and whether a page title confirms where someone has arrived. I like making those details clear enough for a team to examine together.

Away from product work, I run tabletop games. Facilitation, pacing, and adapting to a group's choices are part of that craft. It gives me another place to work with the same ingredients I care about in design: people, structure, and enough room for something unexpected to happen.

How I work with a team

A few decisions that show the working relationship behind the screens.

01 How do you make a handoff useful to engineering?

I connect the visible problem to the behavior we need. In SmartTagIt reviews, I paired issue IDs and severity with short reproduction videos, then annotated the relevant states. Engineering could review the trigger, the screen and the proposed response together.

See the recovery and handoff work
02 When has feedback changed your own design?

My first About Me accomplishments grid left out data that engineering’s table included. I recommended adopting their table and took responsibility for the labels and Figma updates. Their implementation exposed a gap in my mockup; keeping the more complete information mattered more than keeping my layout.

See the About Me design decisions
03 How do you adapt when scope is still moving?

In a September 2026 workflow review, I reused the engineers’ full input fields instead of retaining separate create and edit variants. When a desktop demo skipped existing comment and media options, I asked the product lead to clarify the intended behavior. He confirmed those options should remain. That was an agreed direction, not a verified release.
